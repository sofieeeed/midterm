This is a midterm project from my GES 778: Data Visualization class at UMBC. My undergraduate degree is in Astrobiology & Abiogenesis,
and my capstone project is focused on where microfossils could be preserved on the surface of Mars. For my capstone readers, I wanted a 
visual, data-based case for what kinds of rock to look for on Mars, built from my own analysis rather than solely from the literature.

I used fossil occurrence data from the Paleobiology Database (PBDB) from October 2026, covering the Archean through the Cambrian. I narrowed it to 1,848
occurrences of microbes and simple algae and grouped them by lithology and depositional environment.

I was interested in three questions:
1. Which lithologies (rock types) show early fossil occurrences?
2. Does that mix change across time?
3. Does lithology relate to depositional environment?

For this project, I wanted to improve my annotations, as I found the case study's use of them to be super helpful. I also wanted to 
experiment with a chart that I hadn't created yet, so I chose an alluvial chart. I think my final product turned out great, even though 
I had some bumps along the way. I first had the phylum highlighted, but I realized this made the graph too crowded and actually wasn't 
what my focus was, so I switched to emphasizing which lithologies hold early fossils instead.

After my EDA and final visualizations, I found that:
1. Most of these fossils (59%) are found in carbonate rock, much of it likely built by microbial mats.
2. That isn't true across all of time: the oldest fossils, reaching back about 3.4 billion years, are found in chert, 
even though chemical deposits like chert make up only ~7% of occurrences.
3. Rock type does track environment: reefs are almost all carbonate, chert sits mostly near shore, and deep-water fossils 
are mostly in siliciclastic rock.

For my capstone, I will likely lean into chert and other chemical deposits on Mars rather than carbonate, even though most of 
Earth’s record is found in carbonate. The oldest fossils I found are in chert, and on Earth, much of the carbonate holding these 
fossils was likely built by microbes themselves, so carbonate on Mars, which could have formed without life, may not preserve 
fossils in the same way. These results are based on only a few very old chert records, though, and almost all of this data is marine,
while Jezero was a lake.
